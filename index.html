Sample Audit

What an audit finds: the project management test

To show what this looks like, we ran a pilot of our audit on ClickUp, up against Asana, Monday.com, and Notion. This is a demonstration we ran ourselves, not a client result. The findings below are the real numbers it produced, and they are the kind most brands never see about themselves.

Questions

40 buyer questions

Format

Pilot run, single assistant

Brands tracked

ClickUp, Asana, Monday.com, Notion

Measured

Recommendations, mentions, sentiment

Finding 01

The most recommended brand still has a problem

ClickUp came out on top: it was the recommendation in 10 of the 40 questions, a 25% recommendation rate, more than any other brand in the set.

But ClickUp was also the only brand that picked up negative mentions: 4 of them, and every single one was about complexity. The AI likes the product and warns people about it in the same breath.

Why it mattersWinning the recommendation is only half the story. A warning attached to your name follows buyers into the trial.

Negative mentions, by brand

25% recommendation rate for ClickUp, the best in the set, with a warning attached

Every negative mention in the audit was about one thing: complexity. One clear problem to fix, not a long list of small ones.

Finding 02

Mentioned everywhere, recommended nowhere

Notion showed up in 23 of the 40 answers. It clearly registers: it gets reached for whenever options are being listed.

Then it was recommended zero times. Notion is in the conversation and never the conclusion.

Why it mattersMost visibility tools count mentions and would call this a win. Mentions do not close deals. Recommendations do.

Notion, out of 40 answers

0 times recommended, despite appearing in more than half of all answers
Times mentioned23 of 40
Times recommended0 of 40

Visible in the answers, never the pick. If this is your brand, being famous is not the same as being chosen.

Finding 03

The brand nobody invited owns the word “simple”

Trello was not one of the four brands we set out to track. It showed up anyway, in 11 of the 40 answers, volunteered without being asked about.

Ask for something simple and Trello is the name that comes back. That is why ClickUp's complexity warnings sting: every one of them hands the buyer to Trello.

Why it mattersYour real competitor set is whoever the AI names, not whoever is on your battlecards.

Uninvited appearances

11 answers mentioned Trello, a brand that was not part of the audit
Trello appearances11 of 40

Nobody asked about Trello. It came up on its own, mostly when the question mentioned “simple”.

Finding 04

On price, ClickUp wins outright

The audit included two questions about price, the kind buyers ask when the budget is the deciding factor. ClickUp won both of them.

So the picture is specific, not vague: strong on value, strong on features, held back by one repeated word, complicated.

Why it mattersAn audit that ends with “improve visibility” is useless. An audit that ends with one word to fix is a work plan.
